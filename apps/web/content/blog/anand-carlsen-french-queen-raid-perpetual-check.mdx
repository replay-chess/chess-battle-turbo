## Practical lessons

In the Winawer, forcing <MoveSequencePreview fen={"rnbqk1nr/ppp2ppp/4p3/3p4/1b1PP3/P1N5/1PP2PPP/R1BQKBNR b KQkq - 0 4"} moves={["b4c3"]}>4...Bxc3+</MoveSequencePreview> with a3 is not automatically useful; if White has not fixed the center with e5, Black may get clean counterplay against c3 and c2.

When the opponent's queen is deep in your position, the best compensation is often not a queen chase by itself. Carlsen's key themes were central breaks, rook activity on the g-file, and concrete targets on the c-file.

For White, queen raids work best when the queen has a stable retreat and the center is under control. Stockfish's preferred <MoveSequencePreview fen={"r1b1k1r1/pp1n1p1p/4pn1Q/2p5/q2Pp3/P1P3N1/2PB1PPP/R3KB1R w KQq - 6 12"} moves={["h6e3"]}>12.Qe3</MoveSequencePreview> and <MoveSequencePreview fen={"r1b1k3/pp3p1p/4pnr1/2n5/q3pQ2/P1P3N1/2PB1PPP/R3KB1R w KQq - 0 14"} moves={["c3c4"]}>14.c4</MoveSequencePreview> both show the same lesson: central coordination mattered more than another pawn or a routine developing move.

For Black, the perpetual check pattern with <MoveSequencePreview fen={"r3k3/pp1b1p1p/4p1r1/2Qn4/2B1N3/P1q5/5PPP/R4RK1 b q - 0 19"} moves={["g6g2"]}>19...Rxg2+</MoveSequencePreview>, <MoveSequencePreview fen={"r3k3/pp1b1p1p/4p3/2Qn4/2B1N3/P1q5/5PKP/R4R2 b q - 0 20"} moves={["d5f4"]}>20...Nf4+</MoveSequencePreview>, and <MoveSequencePreview fen={"r3k3/pp1b1p1p/4p3/2Q5/2B1Nn2/P1q5/5P1P/R4RK1 b q - 2 21"} moves={["f4h3"]}>21...Nh3+</MoveSequencePreview> was the tactical payoff. Once the king was boxed between g1 and g2, repetition became the accurate result.
